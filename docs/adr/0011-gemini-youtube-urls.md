# ADR 0011 · Let Gemini read public YouTube videos by URL

**Status** Accepted. Relaxes the content fingerprint of [ADR 0003](0003-worker-recovery.md) for remote media; builds on [ADR 0008](0008-result-reuse.md).

## Context

For Gemini, the worker downloaded the muxed source, re-encoded every 10-minute chunk with libx264, uploaded it to the Files API and deleted it again. Gemini can read a public YouTube video by URL, and `video_metadata` offsets clip the input. A live check on 2026-11-10 confirmed both: a 60-second clip billed 15,780 video and 1,920 audio tokens against 72,325 and 8,791 for the 275-second video, and with the production prompt the transcript covered only the clip with clip-relative timestamps. A loosely worded prompt made the model recite the whole well-known video from memory, so the bounded prompt matters.

## Decision

`ProviderAdapter.remoteMedia` lets a provider accept a resolved source without download. Gemini accepts YouTube sources whose yt-dlp metadata reports `availability: public`, age limit 0, not live or upcoming, a positive duration and an 11-character video ID. It reads `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=<id>` and processes each chunk with `start_offset`/`end_offset`. Everything else keeps the download path. `MEDIA_GEMINI_YOUTUBE_URLS=false` disables remote reading. Gemini labels clipped frames with their position on the source video, so a chunk starting at 9:56 is described from 9:56, not from zero. The prompt names the clip's span on that timeline and asks for `MM:SS` timestamps within it; the provider converts them to clip-relative milliseconds and treats up to one second beyond an edge as the edge, since `MM:SS` has one-second resolution. The upload path uses the same format with a span starting at zero. Output still outside the clip is requested again, up to three calls per chunk, and the worker rejects whatever remains.

Remote media has no local bytes, so its source identity is `remote:<kind>:<site>:<mediaId>` instead of a SHA-256 of the content. Saved checkpoints resume only under the same identity; a job that saved byte checkpoints fails with `source_changed` rather than mixing identities. Duration comes from site metadata. Loudness cannot be measured, so every boundary overlaps and is stitched as in [ADR 0009](0009-pause-aligned-chunks.md).

## Consequences

Remote jobs download nothing and use no local CPU for media; Google fetches the video, at whatever quality it chooses, rather than the 360p muxed format yt-dlp selected. The site ID is trusted as content identity, as result reuse already does. A video replaced under the same ID is not detected. Transcription assumes the video has audio. Retained inspection reports the site duration and assumes audio and video streams, with no byte size. The free Gemini tier limits YouTube input to 8 hours per day. Changing YouTube availability during a job surfaces as a provider error. Live on 2026-11-10, one of four identical calls for a 275-second clip returned timestamps up to 330 seconds and failed the job before the retry existed. The retry was not enough: for a 10-minute chunk of a 14-minute talk, Gemini 2.5 Pro exceeded clip-relative milliseconds in three of three calls, and for the second chunk it answered on the source timeline. With video-timeline `MM:SS`, twelve of twelve calls on both models stayed within their clips, including clips starting mid-video, and spoken words landed within about two to eight seconds of YouTube's captions. Treat Gemini segment timing as approximate.
